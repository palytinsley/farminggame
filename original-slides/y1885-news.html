Central Nebraska  ·  Harvest Edition, 1885

Dry May Withers the Small Grains

Latest harvest news from along the river

❖ A dry spring, then too much rain in July, nearly destroyed the wheat, barley, and oats.

❖ Drought back east pushed up prices for corn, beans, field peas, and tobacco.

❖ Texas ranchers flooded the market with range cattle, so cattle prices fell.

❖ Sheep prices are up. Hog prices hit an all-time high.

1885

“Back east” means east of the Mississippi River.
