The Platte Valley Gazette

Central Nebraska  ·  Harvest Edition, 1886

Killing Winter, Then a Wet Summer

Latest harvest news from along the river

❖ The coldest winter on record, 60 below zero, killed most of the cattle and hogs.

❖ Sheep prices are high.

❖ Plenty of rain grew excellent corn, wheat, barley, and oats. Tobacco and peas held steady.

❖ Late frost cut the bean crop. Hay prices fell with low demand.

1886

History note: the real “Big Die-Up” hit the open range in the winter of 1886–87, with the worst blizzards in January 1887. The game moves it up a year. Losses were so heavy that open-range cattle ranching never fully recovered.
